# Asks between hubs wait in an outbox while the other hub is offline or older

#1066 (hub/T-667), split 2 of T-389. [A board asks a board on another of your hubs while it's online](2026-11-18-2200-a-board-asks-a-board-on-another-of-your-hubs-while-its-online.md) refused an ask, or an answer going back, when the other hub couldn't take it. The owner's T-389 answers: while the other hub is offline, "queue and retry until delivered, no limit"; while it runs an older kickflip, "queue until it updates". This replaces those refusals.

- **One outbox per hub.** `~/.claude/kickflip/peer-outbox.json`, a list, oldest first, of `{id, hub, what: ask | answer, body, at, queued}`. Only the hub writes it, one write at a time (`hub/peers.mjs sendToPeer`, `retryOutbox`); it holds the asks this hub's boards put to other hubs and the answers they send back, so both directions of a conversation queue on the hub doing the sending.
- **What queues.** `/_hub/peers/{ask,answer}` sends at once as before. A peer that doesn't answer (502), one that `tailscale status` lists as the owner's but offline (502, where it used to be "isn't one of your machines online"), and one that answers without JSON, a kickflip from before asks between hubs (501), queue: the route answers `202 {queued: "<hub> is offline" | "<hub> runs an older kickflip"}`. Everything else still refuses at once, since waiting wouldn't change it: the tailnet off here (409) or tailscale silent (503), which the owner fixes here; a name that's this hub (400) or no machine of the owner's (404); and the other hub's own refusal, such as no board at that route (400) or its owner gate (403).
- **The minute job.** `peer-outbox` on the hub's clock, every 4 ticks of 15 s (`shared/polls.mjs`), runs `retryOutbox`: oldest first, a send that goes leaves the outbox; one the peer still can't take keeps its place with this try's line, and holds back the later ones to that hub until the next minute, so a hub that's away costs one timeout a minute, not one per entry. Nothing goes while the tailnet is off here or tailscale is silent. A test calls `hub.outbox()` with the hub's clocks off.
- **A refusal once it's back.** An answer the other hub refuses, such as for a board removed there, is logged and dropped: the answering board already ended its wait. An ask it refuses stays in the outbox marked `refused`, and is never sent again, so `asks` on the asking board reads `not sent: <why>`, and `asks --wait` stops waiting on it.
- **The asking board names the question.** A queued ask has no board-ask event on the other hub yet, so `askBoard` names it with a new event id, sent as `from.id`, recorded in `board-asks.jsonl` under it, and kept in the other board's `board-ask` event; `answerBoardAsk` sends the answer back under `from.id`. A peer from before this change files no `from.id` and answers under its own event id, as before.
- **Delivered at most once.** A send whose reply was lost (a timeout after the other hub took it) is sent again. `takePeerAsk` hands back the board-ask already filed for the same `from.hub` and `from.id`, and `takePeerAnswer` lands nothing for a question whose `board-answer` is already in the asking board's log.
- **What the agents see.** `ask <hub>/<route>` records the question and prints `queued: <hub> is offline, so the question goes once it's back`; the asking task's grey line reads "Asked <hub>/<route>, queued: <hub> is offline: <question>". `asks` reads `queued: <hub> is offline` while the outbox holds it (the outbox's own entry, read on each call), then "waiting on <hub>/<route>'s orchestrator". `answer` records the answer at once, ending the wait, and prints `queued: <hub> is offline, so the answer goes once it's back`. `boards` lists an offline hub as `<hub>: <hub> is offline`.

Considered:
- One file per peer hub. One file in order keeps each hub's sends in order just the same, and the hub reads one file a minute.
- Queuing on the tailnet off here or tailscale silent. Those are this host's own, and refusing says how to fix them; the outbox still waits them out once something is queued.
- Retrying on the 15 s tick. A peer that's away would cost a 10 s timeout every 15 s; the card asks for the minute tick.
- A line on the asking task when a queued ask goes. The answer's message follows on the task anyway; `asks` says where it stands meanwhile.
